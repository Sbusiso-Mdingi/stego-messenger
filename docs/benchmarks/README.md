# Benchmark results

Generated by `python -m src.benchmark`. The sweep embeds random payloads (which are
statistically indistinguishable from the AES-GCM ciphertext the toolkit actually hides)
into a fixed corpus of public-domain images, at a range of payload sizes, repeated over
five seeds. It records distortion (PSNR, SSIM), exact-recovery rate on the lossless PNG
carrier, and the output of two calibrated steganalysis detectors.

Numbers below are from `latest/benchmark.json`: five images
(astronaut, coffee, rocket, chelsea, hubble_deep_field) x six payload sizes x five seeds =
150 embeddings. Confidence intervals are the 95% normal-approximation interval over the
25 observations at each payload size. The exact environment (library versions, platform,
git commit) is recorded in the JSON.

| Payload (of capacity) | PSNR (dB) | SSIM | RS estimated rate [95% CI] | Chi-square prob. | Recovery (PNG) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0% (clean control) | lossless | 1.0000 | 0.028 [0.017, 0.039] | 0.377 | 100% |
| 5% | 64.2 | 0.9998 | 0.076 [0.065, 0.087] | 0.405 | 100% |
| 10% | 61.1 | 0.9996 | 0.120 [0.107, 0.133] | 0.444 | 100% |
| 25% | 57.2 | 0.9989 | 0.254 [0.235, 0.274] | 0.538 | 100% |

## What this shows

**The embedding is visually invisible but statistically obvious.** At a 10% payload the
stego image is 61 dB from its cover with an SSIM of 0.9996 (no visible change), yet RS
analysis recovers the embedding rate as 0.12, cleanly separated from the clean-cover
control at 0.03. The whole point of the toolkit is to measure this gap rather than claim
it away: AES-256-GCM protects the *content* of the message, but sequential LSB embedding
does not hide the *presence* of a payload from a standard detector.

**RS analysis is the reliable estimator; chi-square is a coarse screen.** The RS estimate
tracks the true embedding rate to within a few points across the range and its confidence
intervals are narrow. The Westfeld-Pfitzmann chi-square statistic rises with embedding but
has a high and image-dependent baseline on untouched natural images (0.377 here), so it is
useful as a flag rather than a quantitative estimate. This matches the published behaviour
of both methods.

**Recovery is exact only on the lossless carrier.** Every embedded payload is recovered
bit-for-bit from the PNG. The transformation experiments in `src/attacks.py` and
`evaluate_attacks` show the opposite: JPEG, noise, cropping and resizing destroy the LSB
plane and recovery fails. The toolkit measures that failure rather than assuming
robustness.

## Reproducing

```bash
pip install -e '.[dev]' scikit-image
python -m src.benchmark --out docs/benchmarks/latest
```

The corpus images are regenerated from scikit-image's bundled public-domain samples, so
they are not committed; only the result files are. Pass `--seeds`, `--fractions` and
`--images` to change the sweep.
